The scientist only needs to touch two things on the digital scale: the power button, which turns it on and off, and the tare/zero button, which makes the scale ignore the weight of the beaker. The display screen is the third part of the interface, because it shows the final number in grams. Inside the box, three hidden things happen that the scientist doesn’t need to understand. The load cell is a metal sensor that bends slightly under weight. Signal processing turns a tiny electrical pulse into a digital number. Calibration math makes sure 1 gram on the scale is really 1 gram in the real world. This is abstraction: we only see what we need to use, and the complicated parts stay hidden.
